Day 31

Today, I continued working on the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) project, which is being developed to provide users with accurate and context-aware responses from a knowledge base. My mentor first explained the complete architecture of the RAG system in detail. I understood how a user's query travels through different stages before reaching the Large Language Model (LLM). Instead of directly asking the AI to answer, the system first searches for the most relevant information from the stored documents and then provides that context to the language model, resulting in more reliable and factual responses.

After understanding the workflow, I studied the major components involved in the project, including data ingestion, document preprocessing, embedding generation, vector databases, retrieval mechanisms, and response generation. My mentor explained why embeddings are required instead of simple keyword searching. I also learned how semantic search helps in finding documents based on meaning rather than exact words.

Later, I observed the existing project structure and understood how different modules communicate with each other. The frontend sends the user's question to the backend API, where relevant document chunks are retrieved before generating the final answer. I noted down the sequence of operations to gain a better understanding of the complete workflow.

Towards the end of the day, I discussed the architecture with my mentor and clarified my doubts regarding document retrieval and the role of vector databases. This discussion helped me understand why RAG systems are becoming popular for AI-powered applications.

Learning Outcome:

Understood the complete architecture of the RAG system.
Learned the flow of user queries from frontend to AI response.
Gained knowledge of semantic search, embeddings, and vector databases.
Improved understanding of AI-powered information retrieval.
